The artists

Painters who shaped the visual imagination of the Scottish Highlands.

Horatio McCulloch

1805–1867. Glasgow-born, widely considered the founding father of Scottish landscape painting. His panoramic depictions of Glen Coe, Loch Katrine and Loch Lomond defined the Victorian image of the Highlands. Elected R.S.A. 1838.

Sir Edwin Landseer

1802–1873. English by birth; annual visits to Scotland produced some of the most famous Highland paintings of the 19th century. The Monarch of the Glen made the red deer an enduring symbol of the Highlands.

Peter Graham

1836–1921. Edinburgh-born painter celebrated for his atmospheric studies of Highland weather. Misted glens, cliff-bound seas and cattle on stormy moors. Royal Academician from 1881.

Joseph Farquharson

1846–1935. Known affectionately as "Frozen Mutton Farquharson" for his snowy scenes featuring sheep. Painted the Cairngorms and his family estate at Finzean in Aberdeenshire over seven decades.

William McTaggart

1835–1910. Often called the Scottish Impressionist. Painted the western Highlands and islands with loose, light-filled brushwork that anticipated Modernism. His Skye and Kintyre seascapes remain among the most admired Scottish paintings.

Jacob More

1740–1793. An early Scottish landscape painter whose Falls of Clyde series in the 1770s established the Highland sublime as a subject for serious painting.

Alfred de Bréanski Sr.

1852–1928. English painter of Polish descent whose luminous depictions of the Trossachs and the western lochs were among the most commercially successful Highland paintings of the late Victorian era.

John MacWhirter

1839–1911. A pupil of McCulloch. Atmospheric scenes of Glen Affric, Skye and the Cairngorms. Royal Academician from 1893.
